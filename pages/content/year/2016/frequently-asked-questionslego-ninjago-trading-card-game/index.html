Overview

LEGO Ninjago Trading Card Game[]

Why are the cards in German?

Why are the cards in German?[]

Blue Ocean, the company who produces the game, is based in Germany.[1] Thus, German releases of the game are very widespread/accessible, and have the most prominent advertising online. However, the game is available in various countries outside of Germany, and in a variety of different languages.[2]

How do I play the game? / Where can I find the official rules in English?

How do I play the game? / Where can I find the official rules in English?[]

Official videos explaining the rules in English were produced for Series 1. For later series of the game, some countries (most consistently Hungary) received rules pamphlets (found in every few Booster packs) written in English. Scans of many of the official English rule pamphlets can be found here (note that the images do not initially render at full size; to see the scans in higher resolution, first click on the image, then click on “See full size image”).

Why did they stop selling the cards in the United Kingdom?

Why did they stop selling the cards in the United Kingdom?[]

After Series 3, the cards stopped being sold in the United Kingdom (with the exception of a few XXL cards available with some issues of the Ninjago magazine[3]). Despite numerous unconfirmed theories, the reasoning as to why the cards stopped being sold there is not publicly known. The reason is likely only known internally at Blue Ocean (the company who produces the game) or at Intermediate Media (the company responsible for distributing Blue Ocean’s products in the UK).

Why are the cards not available in North America?

Why are the cards not available in North America?[]

It is not publicly known why the game is not available in North America. The reason is likely only known internally at Blue Ocean (the company who produces the game).

Why don't they make the cards in English?

Why don't they make the cards in English?[]

Despite no longer being sold in any predominantly English-speaking countries, the LEGO Ninjago Trading Card Game is still available in English. Various non-English-speaking countries receive releases for the game where all the cards are in English instead of the local language.[4] Oftentimes, however, the packaging is still in the local language, despite the cards inside being in English.[5]

Some countries received cards in their local language for a few series, only to have their cards switch to English later on.[6] Meanwhile, other countries initially received cards in English, only to later receive cards in their local language.[7] Hungary is the only country to consistently receive cards in English for every single series of the game since Series 3 (the last series to be sold in the UK).[8]

In what countries are the cards available?

In what countries are the cards available?[]

Series of the LEGO Ninjago Trading Card Game have been sold in various countries around the world. However, over the course of the game’s run, various countries have only received certain series of the game, and missed out on others. Various countries received a few series, only to stop receiving releases for the game altogether.[4] That being said, which countries have the game available to them depends on what time the question is being asked. The best way to determine if the current series of cards are being released in a given country is to simply check if they’re for sale at the country’s local magazine shops/supermarkets. Why are some series numbered differently in different countries?

Why are some series numbered differently in different countries?[]

Since some countries received only a few series of the game, and missed out on others, the way the series are numbered in the local version of the game is changed to reflect that. For example, Brazil didn’t receive Series 1 or Series 2, only Series 3. Thus, Series 3 in Brazil is considered Series 1.[9] The Ninjago Wiki uses the numbering as seen in the German and Hungarian releases of the game.

Why do some cards have multiple different numbers?

Why do some cards have multiple different numbers?[]

Some Limited Edition (LE) cards are not released in different countries’ versions of the game. Thus, virtually every single country’s release of any given series has a different selection of Limited Edition cards, and different numbers for those cards.[4] This can even apply to Limited Edition cards of the same language when multiple countries share the language they receive cards in.[10] The Ninjago Wiki generally prioritizes the numbers as seen in the German release, with minor exceptions for Limited Edition cards not present in the German release. The different numbers a Limited Edition card has been assigned in each country it was released in can be found in the infobox on the respective card’s page.
